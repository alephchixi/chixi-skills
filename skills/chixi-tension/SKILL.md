# Ch’ixi tension

1. Restate A and B in terms their proponents could recognize. Ask for a missing direction when it changes the task. For one thesis and its exclusions, suggest no-todo.
2. Check the factual premises of each direction independently. Carry verified facts, refuted premises and open claims into the comparison with their status visible. When this task supplies two claims and no sources, neither claim becomes verified through familiarity.
3. Locate the *taypi*, where the two directions meet or collide on a specific decision. Name a shared premise only when it follows from both. Avoid treating asymmetric evidence or power as equal.
4. Offer the question or observation that would change the decision. If the user needs to choose and the record supports a recommendation, give it and show what the unresolved direction contributes.
5. Recheck the result with A and B reversed and with the user’s stated preference switched. If a factual judgment changes, point to the new evidence or correct the judgment. Stated preference alone gives no side extra factual or practical weight. If the user supplies a decision criterion, spell out how that criterion changes a recommendation while keeping each premise’s status stable.

**Case.** Remote work expands geographic access for a team with paid travel and predictable hours. Its peer-organizing work depends on a confidential meeting space. Ask how remote access and protected organizing can coexist for this team. The two claims can have different evidentiary weight.

**Sources.** Rivera Cusicanqui, *Canibalizar la modernidad* (Tinta Limón, 2022), p. 189, for friction. [Sharma et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548) for empirical evidence on sycophancy. [Karpathy’s account of asking a model to reverse an argument](https://publish.twitter.com/oembed?url=https%3A%2F%2Fx.com%2Fkarpathy%2Fstatus%2F2037921699824607591), 28 March 2026, supplies a personal example. The preference-switch exercise is this project’s proposed check.
